## Adjusted-price convention

Adjusted closes were verified, not assumed. Reconstructing them from raw closes and the dividend record under Yahoo's back-adjustment convention, `r_t = Close_t / (Close_{t-1} - Div_t) - 1`, reproduces the adjusted series for all four instruments (deviations below). The naive `(Close_t + Div_t) / Close_{t-1}` convention does not match and is not used. GLD and SLV pay no distributions, so their adjusted and raw series coincide.

Yahoo's adjusted prices are back-adjusted and anchored to the latest distribution, so adjusted *levels* shift between downloads as new distributions arrive, while returns inside a fixed download do not. The raw download is archived and checksummed for this reason.

Yahoo's action record contains a spurious 10:1 SLV split on 2008-07-24. The price series is continuous across that date and the adjusted series does not apply it, so the pipeline takes split handling from the adjusted series only and never applies the `Stock Splits` column. This is checked below and in `test_data.py`.

## Transformations applied

The complete list, in order. Nothing else is done to the data in Phase 1.

1. Download raw OHLCV, `Adj Close`, `Dividends` and `Stock Splits` per ticker; archive verbatim as CSV.
2. Select the `Adj Close` column of each ticker as its total-return price series.
3. Restrict to 2007-01-03..2026-09-30, plus the single preceding trading day as the return base.
4. Join the four series on their common trading calendar (no reindexing, no filling).
5. Compute daily simple returns between consecutive observed trading days.
