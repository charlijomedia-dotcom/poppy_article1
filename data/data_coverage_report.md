## Risk-free rate and cash returns

FRED series `DGS3MO` (3-Month Treasury Constant Maturity Rate), published as an annualised percentage on an investment basis. Converted to a daily rate as `(1 + y/100) ** (1/252) - 1`, the constant daily rate compounding to the quoted annual rate over a 252-day year.

* Trading days covered: 4967
* Annualised yield range: 0.00% to 5.63%
* Mean daily rate: 0.00006252
* Trading days with no published quote: **36** (1.83 per year)

The bond market closes on Columbus Day and Veterans Day while the NYSE trades, so those days carry no published quote. The last previously published yield is carried forward, which uses only past information. This is a convention on the level of an interest rate, not an invented price observation, and each occurrence is flagged in the `carried_forward` column of `data/processed/risk_free_daily.csv`. Phase 2 uses the rate dated *t-1* for the cash return earned on day *t*, so no yield enters a decision before it was published.

## Adjusted-price convention

Adjusted closes were verified, not assumed. Reconstructing them from raw closes and the dividend record under Yahoo's back-adjustment convention, `r_t = Close_t / (Close_{t-1} - Div_t) - 1`, reproduces the adjusted series for all four instruments (deviations in the check table). The naive `(Close_t + Div_t) / Close_{t-1}` convention does not match and is not used. GLD and SLV pay no distributions, so their adjusted and raw series coincide.

Yahoo's adjusted prices are back-adjusted and anchored to the latest distribution, so adjusted *levels* shift between downloads as new distributions arrive, while returns inside a fixed download do not. The raw download is archived and checksummed for this reason, and the default build verifies those checksums rather than re-downloading.

### Share splits

Both of Yahoo's price columns, `Close` and `Adj Close`, are back-adjusted for share splits. The pipeline therefore takes split handling from those columns and **never applies the `Stock Splits` column itself**, so each split is applied exactly once.

* **SLV, 2008-07-24, 10-for-1.** iShares Silver Trust 10-for-1 share split, reducing the silver backing from approximately 10 ounces to approximately 1 ounce per share. Source: SEC Form 10-Q, iShares Silver Trust (CIK 0001330568): https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1330568/000119312509106967/d10q.htm

The adjusted series is continuous across this date, which is the expected consequence of the adjustment already being present: an unadjusted series would fall by about 90% on the split date, and a doubly-adjusted one would rise tenfold. Continuity is therefore evidence that the adjustment is applied once, not evidence that no split occurred, and it is checked as such.

## Transformations applied

The complete list, in order. Nothing else is done to the data in Phase 1.

1. Acquire raw OHLCV, `Adj Close`, `Dividends` and `Stock Splits` per ticker, plus the raw FRED yield series; archive each verbatim and checksum it.
2. Select the `Adj Close` column of each ticker as its total-return price series (already split- and distribution-adjusted; the `Stock Splits` column is never applied).
3. Restrict to 2007-01-03..2026-09-30, plus the single preceding trading day as the return base.
4. Join the four series on their common trading calendar (no reindexing onto a constructed calendar, no filling).
5. Compute daily simple returns between consecutive observed trading days.
6. Align the Treasury yield to those trading days, carry the last published yield forward on bond-market holidays (flagged), and convert to a daily rate via `(1 + y/100) ** (1/252) - 1`.
